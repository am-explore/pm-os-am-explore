am-explore field map

The Product Line

A route map through the product development lifecycle — every phase, and the AI skills, reviewer agents, and templates that live there. Two lines run through it: PM OS, the day-to-day product lifecycle, and Agent Production Readiness, which interchanges where a team is shipping an AI agent specifically.

The PM OS line

1

Intake

pm-os-am-explore

Every request gets one front door and one answer — even if the answer is "not now" or "no."

Skill /intake Separates the problem from the proposed solution, then routes: fast-track, discover, evaluate, park, decline
Template intake-request.md Verbatim ask, four-slot problem statement, tagged evidence, routing decision, reply to requester
Log intake/ Register of every request and where it went — parked items carry a revisit trigger

Routes to Discover when the problem isn't understood yet, or to Trade-off & Evaluate when it competes for capacity. Repeated declines become anti-bets in Define.

2

Discover

pm-os-am-explore

Before you scope anything: what's the problem, and whose problem is it.

Skill /discover Opportunity Solution Tree — research signal → validated opportunity
Skill /interview-prep User interview guide, built as a safari not a validation session
Reviewer user-researcher Critiques evidence quality and assumption risk
Watcher risk-monitor Scans early for dependency risk and blockers
Routine user-feedback-synthesis Wednesday 10am — aggregates incoming feedback into themes

Feeds context-library/users.md — personas, JTBD, and research get written back here.

3

Trade-off & Evaluate

pm-os-am-explore

The problem is understood and credible. Now: what does choosing it cost, and what are you betting on.

Skill /evaluate Options, criteria weighted before scoring, and an explicit statement of what is given up
Template tradeoff-evaluation.md Weighted scoring, sensitivity check on unvalidated inputs, the bet, the kill criterion
Template roi-impact-estimation.md Business case for the option you chose, before committing engineering time
Debate /review-strategy For one-way-door calls: reviewers critique, then the real trade-off is surfaced for you to decide
Log decision-log/ The decision and its reasoning, recorded with the strongest counter-argument

Hands the chosen option to Define & Strategize. Options that keep losing for the same reason are proposed there as anti-bets.

Reviewer legal-reviewer Compliance risk and data handling flags
Debate /review-prd All 7 reviewers critique, then debate-facilitator + synthesis-agent weigh in
Hook prd-quality-gate Advisory check that fires after a PRD is drafted

Build & Ship

pm-os-am-explore

Design's validated. This is where execution discipline and decision hygiene matter most.

Skill /setup-routine Turn a recurring build-phase task into a scheduled routine

Reviewer engineer-reviewer Feasibility, tech debt, implementation risk
Log decision-log/ Institutional memory — every decision, with rationale

Drift Watch

agent-production-readiness-framework

Runs every session, not just one station

These aren't phase-bound — they're read or checked at the start of every session, regardless of where you are on the line.

Brain CLAUDE.md Hub file — routes triggers to skills, agents, and templates

Rules operating-principles.md Context over commands, artifacts over chat, closed-world grounding
Rules output-standards.md Quality bars per output type, plus the Epistemic Tagging convention
Watcher context-updater Flags stale context-library entries
Hook context-freshness-check Session-start check that context hasn't gone stale
Context context-library/ Company, product, users, competitors, team, metrics

Built by Akash Mohan. pm-os-am-explore is a customization of agentmart/pm-os (MIT). agent-production-readiness-framework is Apache 2.0.

Two ideas layered on top of that base, credited to where they were first seen (concepts, not copied text): the Epistemic Tagging convention ([Fact] / [Inference] / [Assumption]) is inspired by the fact/inference/assumption labeling in deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills; the Closed-World Constraint Check in hooks/prd-quality-gate.md is inspired by the closed-world validation approach in vishalmdi/ai-native-pm-os.

Two more, from Shubhamsaboo/awesome-llm-apps (Apache 2.0; concepts, not copied code): /skill-evals follows the keep-if-better loop of the Self-Improving Agent Skills example, which itself builds on Andrej Karpathy's autoresearch; /panel follows the two-round blind review of the LLM Panel Agent Team example by Jaret Arnold.
