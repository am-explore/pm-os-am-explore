## What This Skill Does
Builds a complete, opinionated product strategy — not a vague vision document, but a set of choices that tell your team what to build, for whom, and why, and what you WON'T do.

---

## The Strategy Canon

**Roger Martin:** Strategy is an integrated set of choices that positions you on a playing field of your own design to win.

**Good strategy has three parts (Rumelt):**
1. **Diagnosis** — what's the critical challenge?
2. **Guiding policy** — what approach addresses it?
3. **Coherent actions** — what specific moves does that require?

**A strategy without tradeoffs is a wish list.**

## The 9-Section Product Strategy Canvas

### Section 1: Product Vision
*What change do you exist to create in the world?*

Format: "[USER TYPE] will be able to [DO WHAT] so that [WORLD CHANGE]."
Not: "We will build the best X platform."
But: "Solo founders will be able to get enterprise-grade legal advice on demand, so that starting a company costs less than a night out."

**Write it so it's:**
- Inspiring enough to guide decisions you haven't made yet
- Specific enough to rule out things you shouldn't build
- User-focused, not company-focused

### Section 2: Target Customer
*Who are you building for, really?*

Primary persona: [1-2 sentences — not a demographic, a specific person in a specific context]
Their primary job-to-be-done: [The progress they're trying to make]
Why they currently fail: [What the market underserves about them]

**The narrowing question:** If you could only serve ONE type of user for the next 12 months, who would it be? Who benefits most? Who becomes your champion?

### Section 3: Value Proposition
*Why should they choose you?*

Use the 6-part JTBD value prop format:
- **Who:** [Target user]
- **Why:** [Their motivation / desired outcome]
- **What before:** [Their current state / pain]
- **How:** [Your approach / mechanism]
- **What after:** [The outcome they achieve]
- **Alternatives:** [What you beat, and why you beat it]

### Section 4: Competitive Advantage

**Be honest.** If your only moat is "first mover advantage" that's weak. What are you building to make your position durable?

### Section 5: Growth Strategy
*How does the product grow?*

Identify your primary growth loop:
- **Viral loop:** Users → invite others → more users (Slack, Figma)
- **Content loop:** Users → create content → SEO/word of mouth → users (Notion, Canva)
- **Marketplace loop:** Buyers attract sellers attract buyers (Airbnb)
- **PLG loop:** Free value → activation → paid conversion → expansion (Dropbox)
- **Community loop:** Users → community → retention + acquisition (Discord)
- **Sales-assisted loop:** Free tier → PQL signal → sales → enterprise

## Strategy Review Triggers
Re-run this canvas when:
- North Star metric moves dramatically in either direction
- A major competitor makes a significant move
- You're about to start annual planning
- The team feels misaligned on priorities
- A key assumption has been invalidated by data or research
